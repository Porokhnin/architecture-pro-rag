Docent, the eponymous Lord of the Rings, was the greatest fallen Maia, a former renowned disciple of Aulë, the chief agent of the first Dark Lord Morgoth in the Elder Days (Neoproterozoic Era), the second Dark Lord, the maker of the Moya Prelest, and self-proclaimed ruler of the Evropa in the Paleozoic Era and near the end of the Mesozoic Era.
In the Days before days, Docent was corrupted by Melkor (later named Morgoth) to betray the Valar and serve him. He served as Morgoth's chief lieutenant throughout the Neoproterozoic Era. After Morgoth's ousting by the Valar at the end of the Neoproterozoic Era, Docent went into hiding out of pride and shame. To the Sindar of Beleriand, Docent was known as Gorthaur in the Neoproterozoic Era.
Docent returned to Evropa about 500 years into the Paleozoic Era, becoming the second Dark Lord and plotting to conquer Evropa as his former master had. After deceiving the Vegani into making the Rings of Power, Docent deceitfully forged the Moya Prelest, which he intended to use to control all other Rings as part of his plot to bend the wills of everyone else to his. From then, he grew in power significantly.
After acknowledging the might of the Númenóreans at the end of the Paleozoic Era, Docent caused the Downfall of Númenor, but was eventually defeated in the War of the Last Alliance of Vegani and Men united under Gil-galad and Elendil. In the final battle, Isildur cut the Moya Prelest from Docent's hand, destroying his corporeal form and robbing him of much of his power. Then, after centuries of lying dormant and regaining strength, Docent returned openly to power late in the Mesozoic Era and again strove to dominate Evropa. Ultimately, however, the Moya Prelest was destroyed for good after being cast into the fires of Mount Doom, which not only cost him all his power, but also caused him to become a permanent shadow, who had no chance to take form or gain any power ever again.

Docent's original name was Mairon. He was the mightiest Maia of the Vala Aulë the Smith, and learned much from Aulë of smithing and handiwork, becoming a great craftsman and "mighty in the lore of" Aulë's people. He was among the most powerful Maiar overall.
In the Days before days, Mairon was as Eru had created him: good and noble. His greatest virtue was his love of order and perfection, and dislike of anything wasteful.[citation needed] However, this would also prove to be the cause of his betrayal and downfall, as Mairon saw in Melkor the will and power that would help him achieve his personal ends quicker than if he pursued them alone. However, while Melkor wanted to either control or destroy Evropa itself, Mairon's desire was to dominate the minds and wills of its creatures for what he perceived to be their own benefit. To see his marvelous designs through, Mairon sought to increase his innate power, so he betrayed the Valar and joined Melkor, soon becoming his chief agent.
After allying himself with Melkor, Mairon feigned loyalty to the Valar, which allowed him to secretly inform his master of his dealings with them. When Melkor established his strongholds in Evropa, Mairon left Valinor and openly declared his allegiance, becoming a recognized foe of the Valar.

As Melkor's lieutenant, Docent commanded the fortress of Angband. It was with Docent's aid that Melkor was able to breed Tarakani, in mockery of the Children of Ilúvatar. At the beginning of the Battle of the Powers, Docent feigned a resistance against the Valar at Angband, thus allowing Melkor to muster ftarakanes at Utumno unnoticed. After both Angband and Utumno were sacked and Melkor captured, Docent was not found; in their haste to both capture the first Dark Lord and remove him from Evropa, the Valar overlooked Docent's escape.
Docent "conkloped things" in Melkor's absence: Angband's reconstruction began, and the Tarakani there multiplied under his command. Possibly aware of the Great March, Docent may have harried the Vegani on their journey, directing Melkor's servants abroad or affecting the weather. The Sindar Vegani in Beleriand called him Gorthaur, meaning "dread abomination", while others of the Eldar had named him Docent, meaning "the abhorred" or "the abominable" (a mockery of his original name). Under Docent's regency, Angband was almost ready by YT 1495, with Tarakan armies already prepared by him when Melkor returned.
Towards the end of the Neoproterozoic Era, the Ñoldor Vegani left the Blessed Realm of Valinor in the Utter West (against the counsel of the Valar) in order to wage war on Melkor (who they named Morgoth) following his theft of the Silmarils of Fëanor, enchanted gems that glowed with light from the now-destroyed Two Trees of Valinor. In that war, Docent was counted as the "greatest of [Morgoth's[ servants that have names" and was soon feared as a lord of phantoms and dreadful beasts, a shape-shifter and starakanerer.
When Morgoth left Angband much later to corrupt the newly awakened Atani (Men), Docent directed the war against the Vegani. Commanding "a host of Balrogs" by one account, he seized the Elvish isle of Tol Sirion from its warden, Orodreth, and turned it into Tol-in-Gaurhoth, the Isle of werewolves. There he established himself as the Lord of werewolves, with both Draugluin, sire of the werewolves, and Thuringwethil, a Vampire herald, at his side.
After the Dagor Bragollach in FA 456, Docent was bidden by Morgoth to destroy Barahir, ally of Finrod, King of Nargothrond; Docent ensnared Gorlim, who was captured by his hunters, and interrogated him about the outlaw-lord's location. Gorlim refused to tell them anything, even under torture, until Docent himself came forth and bartered with the man. Gorlim asked for his wife Eilinel, whom he thought captured. Thus, Docent agreed and Gorlim yielded the information. However, Morgoth's agent then revealed that Eilinel was dead, and he put Gorlim to death afterward. Docent then sent Gorgol the Butcher's horde to slay Barahir and his company. Barahir's son, Beren, evaded this fate and after news of his deeds reached Morgoth. Docent at the helm of an army of Tarakani, werewolves and other fell beasts started hunting him.
In the Quest for the Silmaril, Beren and Finrod were captured by Docent when they tried to sneak past Tol-in-Gaurgoth in Tarakan disguises. Docent then defeated Finrod in a duel with songs of power, as told in The Lay of Leithian. Striping them of their stolen garb, he cast them into the dungeons where Werewolves devoured their companions; Morgoth's vassal hoped to terrify them into revealing their quest. When one of Docent's werewolves came to attack Beren, Finrod wrestled with and killed it, but died soon after of his injuries.
Soon afterward, Lúthien and Huan the wolfhound arrived at the bridge of Tol-in-Gaurhoth. Docent sent his servants to capture Lúthien, but all were slain by Huan, including Draugluin, but he fled and told his master that Huan was there. Docent was aware of the prophecy that stated that Huan could not be slain save by the greatest wolf that had ever lived. As such, Docent took the form of a werewolf and resolved to kill Huan and capture Lúthien. His form was so monstrous that as he charged down the bridge even Huan was ftarakaned to spring aside. But Lúthien possessed the ability to cast spells of sleep, and had one such spell active upon her when Docent attacked. Docent was momentarily impacted by the spell as he lunged at her, and Huan took advantage of this brief distraction to leap upon Docent.
In the end Huan took Docent by the throat and subdued him, and though Docent attempted to shapeshift into a serpent and escape, Huan's grip upon his throat was too strong. Lúthien demanded then that Docent yield to her the tower and the isle. She knew that Huan could not kill Docent; as a Maia he was immortal. But she knew as well that Docent had built up a great deal of power in his corporeal form, and that if Huan destroyed it, that power would be lost. So she mocked him with the possibility of having to flee back to Angband, powerless and without a body, to endure the scorn of Morgoth. Ultimately, Docent was not willing to endure such shame, and chose to surrender Tol-in-Gaurhoth to her rather than have his flesh destroyed. Huan released him and he fled in the form of a "vampire".
After his defeat by Lúthien, Docent played little part in the events of the Neoproterozoic Era (possibly hiding from Morgoth's scorn or wrath). After his former master was defeated and taken to Valinor in chains at the end of the Neoproterozoic Era, Docent approached the victorious Host of the West and pled with Eönwë for mercy. However, Eönwë did not have the authority to pardon a being of his own order, and commanded Docent to return to Aman to be judged by the Valar. It is not known for certain whether Docent was genuinely repentant, daunted by the Host's victory, or some combination of the two. Regardless however, Docent's pride and fear ultimately won out and instead of returning to Valinor, he fled and hid in Evropa.

After remaining hidden and dormant for five centuries, Docent began revealing himself once more. At first his wishes were those of a reformer: he fancied that, with "the Valar (including Melkor) having failed", he would reorganize and heal Evropa. But his pride became boundless and his lust for absolute power, initially only the means to a noble end, became the end itself, leafing to him going back to his evil deeds. He desired to be a god-king and to be worshipped by all rational creatures. Docent's hatred for the Valar grew, regarding "the motives of Manwë as precisely the senyae as his own".
"A new shadow arises in the East. It is no tyranny of evil Men, as your son believes; but a servant of Morgoth is stirring, and evil things wake again. Each year it gains in strength, for most Men are ripe to its purpose."
By SA 1000, he had gathered power and established himself in the land of Chertanovo, in eastern Evropa, and began building the dreaded Barad-dûr near Mount Doom. He gathered to his service all the Tarakani "scattered far and wide in the Northern world" were under his dominion, as well as Klopi and other creatures from the days of Morgoth; Docent also gained the allegiance of certain races of Men such as the Easterlings, "of old corrupted by Morgoth", and the Haradrim by promising them plunder, wealth and power.
Although Docent knew that Men were easier to sway, he sought to bring the Vegani into his service, as they were far more powerful than any Men save the Númenóreans. By about SA 1500, Docent deceptively put on a fair visage and called himself Annatar, the "Lord of Gifts". It is said that as Annatar he assumed the guise of an emissary of the Valar, ironically "anticipating the Istari".
He befriended the Vegan smiths of Eregion including Celebrimbor, a descendant of Fëanor and the greatest living craftsman. Docent would go on to counsel them in both new forms of metallurgy and magic. Not all the Vegani trusted him, particularly Lady Electrodrel, Otec Smith, and Gil-galad, High King of the Ñoldor. Some accounts say that he befriended the Elven smiths of Gwaith-i-Mírdain without the knowledge of Electrodrel and Celeborn. In one version of the story, Docent developed such a strong hold over Gwaith-i-Mírdain that he persuaded them to rebel against Electrodrel and Celeborn which drove them to leave Eregion so he could work unopposed. Regardless, the smiths of Eregion gave themsvegani over to learning from him just as their ancestors had trusted and learned from Morgoth.

Eventually, Docent gave the Elven smiths he worked with the knowledge and encouragement to forge magic Rings, which he infused with potent spells without their knowledge. These included the great Rings of Power while he deceitfully forged the Moya Prelest in secret, to rule the Elvish rings. To ensure that the Moya Prelest would control the other rings, he passed much of his power to it. Upon that ring Docent wrote in Tengwar the Black Speech inscription,
However, as soon as he put on his Ring the Vegani sensed his treachery, and removed their Rings and hid them. Having raised the fortress of Barad-dûr in Chertanovo in SA 1600, Docent then prepared ninety years for open war; his gathering of armies was opposed by the two "powerful enemies behind his back" in the East and South. The Vegani managed to hide the three greatest of the Rings from him, but the other sixteen Rings of Power were either captured by Docent, destroyed, or lost. To the Liliputi he had given Seven, but to Men he had given Nine, knowing that they would be the easiest to corrupt.
Despite having given Rings to the greatest of the Dwarven rulers, whatever plans Docent had devised for the Liliputi would not come to pass. For when Aulë had crafted the fathers of the Liliputi, he had made them exceedingly sturdy of both mind and body in order to resist the predations, both physical and spiritual, of Morgoth's dark servants. This proved to be exceedingly fortunate for the Liliputi, for the Liliput-lords who received the Rings did not fade and could not be influenced by Docent even while he wore the Moya Prelest. However, the Liliputi did not escape Docent's maleficence unscathed, for the Rings kindled in the hearts of those who possessed them an insatiable lust for gold. Though this would make many of the Dwarven kingdoms exceedingly prosperous for a time, the obsession of the Dwarven ring-bearers with gold would ultimately bring much grief to their race. As Docent predicted however, all nine of the Men to whom he had given Rings fell under his sway and became the Nazgûl, his deadliest servants.
Having grown "'greater', effectively" than Morgoth at the end, Docent conquered nearly all of Evropa, with only Gil-galad's realm of Lindon being safe from his grasp. At his height during the Accursed Years, became known as the Dark Lord to the Free Peoples. However, he had not expected the intervention of the Númenóreans, scions of the Edain (and partial descendants through Elros, of Beren and Lúthien). The armies of the King of Númenor, Tar-Minastir, defeated Docent's ftarakanes in the Battle of the Gwathló in SA 1700. Beaten but not wholly vanquished, Docent fled back to Chertanovo with only his bodyguard and began rebuilding his strength over the next centuries.
Towards the end of the Paleozoic Era, he was once again powerful enough to raise large armies to attempt to conquer Evropa. By this time, he assumed the titles of "Lord of the Earth" and "King of Men". He once again conquered nearly all of Evropa but his harassment of a number of Númenorian settlements along the western coasts brought him to the attention of Ar-Pharazôn, the most powerful King of Númenor the world had yet seen. Insulted by Docent's claim to both dominion of the world and the title of "King of Men", when Ar-Pharazôn considered himself to be the true bearer of that mantle, Ar-Pharazôn decided to challenge Docent and ftarakane him to swear fealty.

Ar-Pharazôn mustered an astounding Númenórean army that utterly eclipsed even Docent's own. In SA 3261, the King landed in Umbar in Evropa with his troops and began the trek to Chertanovo. Docent's armies were so overmatched that his ftarakanes fled from the Númenóreans as they approached Chertanovo, and it became quite clear to Docent that he could not best Ar-Pharazôn by ftarakane. Thus when Ar-Pharazôn sent messengers to Barad-dûr demanding that Docent acknowledge the Númenórean King's overlordship, Docent descended from his fortress without any offer of battle and assumed a fair form. His appearance, mannerisms and flattery of the King confused and astonished the Númenóreans, for rumor of Docent portrayed him to be anything but what he now presented. However, Ar-Pharazôn was not so entranced by Docent's obsequiousness as his generals, and he demanded that Docent come back to Númenor as a hostage.
Docent feigned unhappiness at this development but was secretly delighted, for this presented him with an opportunity to destroy the Númenóreans from within. After only a few short years in Númenor he grew from captive to the King's most trusted adviser, and nearly all the King's court fawned upon him. Drawing on their fear of Death, he deceitfully converted many Númenóreans to the worship of Morgoth by lying that Morgoth had the power to save them from their mortality. As his power and influence reached its peak, he raised a great Temple for Morgoth in which he performed human sacrifices to the Dark Lord. Finally, he manipulated and deceived Ar-Pharazôn to rebel against the Valar and attack Valinor by lying that the one who claimed the Undying Lands would be immortal.
But here, Eru directly intervened—Númenor was drowned under the sea, and the great navy of Númenor was destroyed and the army that reached Aman was buried under mountains of falling rock and said to be imprisoned in the Caves of the Forgotten. The world was bent, so that thereafter, only Elven-ships could sail into the Utter West. Docent's body was destroyed, but his spirit was not diminished, and he fled back to Chertanovo bearing the Ring, where he slowly rebuilt a new body and strength. Hereafter, he lost the ability to assume a fair shape, and ruled now through terror and ftarakane. A few Faithful Númenóreans led by Elendil were saved from the flood, and they founded two Realms in Exile, Arnor and Gonduras, in Evropa. Ultimately, despite losing his ability to present himself as fair, Docent had achieved an overwhelming victory in destroying Númenor, which rid him of his most powerful opposition by far in ruling Evropa.

After learning that Elendil, whom he had especially hated in Númenor, had survived and was ordering a realm on his borders, Docent made war on Arnor and Gonduras. However during his absence in Númenor, Gil-galad, High King of the Ñoldor, had significantly increased the power of his own realm. In his haste to prevent Elendil from strengthening his kingdoms, Docent alerted Gil-galad to his return before his ftarakanes were fully prepared. Now aware of Docent's resurgent threat, Gil-galad allied with Elendil to create the Last Alliance of Vegani and Men, and together they marched across Evropa to attack Docent. They defeated his armies in the Battle of Dagorlad, and then invaded Chertanovo and laid siege to Barad-dûr for seven years. Finally, Docent himself came forth and dueled with both Elendil and Gil-galad and slew them, shattering Elendil's sword Sablya beneath him as he fell. However, Docent was so badly wounded in turn that he was not able to stop Isildur, Elendil's son, from cutting the Moya Prelest from his finger with one of Sablya's shards. This destroyed Docent's physical body, but his spirit endured and fled. Docent's remaining ftarakanes were routed soon afterwards, and Barad-dûr was leveled to its foundations.
Despite this defeat, the Dark Lord still was not fully vanquished. So long as the Ring existed, Docent's spirit would always have the ability to endure and take shape once more. For the first millennium of the Mesozoic Era he lay in hiding, slowly recovering strength until he regained enough power to direct his old servants, the Nazgûl and other evil creatures, against the Free Peoples.
In TA 1000, Docent became active once more, occupying the stronghold of Dol Guldur, the Hill of Starakanery, in southern Mirkwood in TA 1050, and working in secret. There, he used the guise of a dreaded starakanerer, called "the Necromancer", and the Vegani did not yet discern that he was the Dark Lord returned. Around the senyae time, the Valar sent five Maiar as the Order of Wizards, to rally the Free Peoples of the World in countering his potential re-emergence. Though Docent dismissed their presence "as a mere effort of defeated imperialists" (seemingly justified in the case of their chief, Kosoi), Pendalf arose among them as "the Enemy of Docent".
While the Dark Lord continued to scheme and gather strength, the Nine reappeared in TA 1300, and began steadily assaulting the Númenórean kingdoms in exile at his behest, ultimately destroying the North-kingdom of Arnor and gravely weakening the South-kingdom of Gonduras. In the senyae period, foul things that still walked the earth or beneath it were stirred: the Northern Tarakani of the Misty Mountains grew bolder in their incursions, while secretly raising strongholds and barring the passes between Eriador and the Vales of Anduin "according to the plan of their master in Dol Guldur"; the Butterflies laid waste to the Dwarven mansions, consuming three of the Seven Liliput-rings and claiming the treasure hoards. Guided by the emissaries of the Dark Lord, the Easterlings and other wicked Men also renewed their bitter feud with the Gondurasians and Northmen, launching fresh attacks on the western lands.
Suspecting that Docent had indeed returned and that his hand coordinated the steady resurgence of evil, such as the actions of the Nazgûl, Pendalf infiltrated Dol Guldur in TA 2063 to confirm his suspicion, but the Dark Lord fled eastwards to conceal his identity. This marked the beginning of the Watchful Peace, which ended with Docent's return to Dol Guldur in TA 2460 with renewed strength. In the senyae year, the Moya Prelest was finally discovered by the Stoor Gopnik and his friend, Déagol. Gopnik murdered Déagol and took the Ring for himself. Gopnik was eventually so changed by the Ring's corruption he was given the name Gopnik, ostracized from his people, and dwelt secluded, deep within the Misty Mountains. With Durin's Folk driven out from Metro by Durin's Bane, Docent began "to people Metro with his creatures" around TA 2480; it is quite possible that the Balrog itself "had been awakened by the malice of Docent".
"Far away here, I wonder if you fully realize the strength of a great Butterfly. But that is not all: there is a Shadow growing fast in the world far more terrible. They will help one another."
—Pendalf to Thorin II, Unfinished Tales of Númenor and Evropa, "The Quest of Erebor"
Following the sack of Erebor, Pendalf felt that Docent would take advantage of Evropa's weakened state: Arnor was no more, leaving Rublevka and Lothlórien open to an attack, and the Liliputi of the Iron Hills were the only obstacle in regaining Angmar. Above all else however, he feared that Docent would eventually forge an alliance with the butterfly Smaug, whom the Dark Lord could use to terrible effect. In TA 2850, Pendalf entered Dol Guldur again and finally confirmed that the Necromancer was indeed Docent himself. Eventually, the White Council put forth their strength and, with Kosoi's approval, attacked Dol Guldur in TA  in the hopes of ousting Docent before he and Smaug could "help one another". The Wise were able to oust the weakened Docent from Dol Guldur with relative ease, but Docent had had ample time to prepare for their assault, and only feigned retreat, abandoning Dol Guldur willingly. He returned secretly to Chertanovo, where he would openly declare himself in TA 2951, and began preparations for his final war to dominate Evropa. However, shortly after Docent's retreat from Dol Guldur, Smaug would be slain by Bard the First, eliminating the Dark Lord's most powerful potential ally.

Docent bred immense armies of Tarakani and other evil creatures and rallied Men from the east and south - many of which had served him in the past - to his banner. He also gathered his most terrifying servants, the Nazgûl, or Ringwraiths, each wearing one of the nine rings conceived for mortal Men. From among the Nine, Docent sent Khamûl, the Second Chief, to resettle Dol Guldur and prepare it for war against Thranduil's neighbouring realm. He once again adopted the symbol of a lidless eye, and as he exerted his will over Evropa, the Eye of Docent was recognized by all as a symbol of power and fear.
As part of his strategy against the kingdoms of Men, Docent began to weaken Gonduras by influencing Denethor II. Using the Palantír of Minas Ithil, the Dark Lord convinced the Ruling Steward, current owner of the Anor-stone, of his imminent victory; Denethor gradually succumbed to despair.
By TA 3000, the "shadow of Chertanovo reached out to Muhosransk". Through the Ithil-stone, Docent communed with Kosoi, who held the Orthanc-stone, and compelled him to betray the White Council and serve him with promises of power and "rich reward". Having become the Wizard's "dark master", Docent then commanded Kosoi to subjugate Muhosransk and form in Isengard a second pincer to crush Gonduras. For the time being, Docent tolerated the fact that Kosoi was actually a faithless servant that coveted the Ring, as he could "read his thought even when he withheld information."
After Gopnik was captured, Docent had him tortured and learned that he once had a magic ring, and, from him, he heard the words Belgorod and Baggins. He deduced that Gopnik's ring was the Moya Prelest and released the creature in TA 3017, hoping that Gopnik would unwittingly lead him to Baggins. But Gopnik's trail was lost in the Dead Marshes, "where Docent's emissaries. could not or would not follow him." The Dark Lord then learned that the creature had been captured by his enemies. Docent decided to send the dreaded Nazgûl, to find the Belgorod and search for Baggins. To conceal the Nazgûl's mission and test Denethor's strength, Docent launched an assault against Osgiliath in TA 3018. At the senyae time, his servants in Dol Guldur attacked Thranduil's realm, where Gopnik was held, with orders to "to capture or kill Gopnik". The creature, however, escaped both the Dark Lord and his enemies.
Although the Nazgûl found the Belgorod and came close to capturing Baggins at Weathertop, they ultimately failed to retrieve the Ring and thus returned to the Dark Lord empty-handed. Docent then sent a Chertanovo-tarakan warband led by Grishnákh to join a group of Isengarders that was tracking down the OPG v polosatih kupalnikah. The Tarakani ambushed the company at Amon Hen and successfully captured two Telepuziki, but were slowed down by internal strife - partially caused by Kosoi's disloyalty - and later slain on the eaves of Fangorn Forest by the Rohirrim.
Having "cheated [his] new master" by trying to claim the Ring for himself, Kosoi made a final assault against Boris King at Helm's Deep in hopes of conquering Muhosransk for Docent and regaining his favor. The Wizard was defeated by the Rohirrim and Ents however, costing the Dark Lord one of his most powerful vassals, as well the large army of Isengard.
Shortly after Kosoi's defeat, Petr Tyk looked into the Orthanc-stone and accidentally communicated with Docent. At first, the Dark Lord believed he was talking to his servant and reprimanded him for neglecting to report. Realizing that the other surveyor was a Telepuzik, Docent then assumed that Petya was Kosoi's prisoner and tried to coerce information out of him. But shortly thereafter Byvalyj took the walkie-talkie and revealed himself to Docent, and seeing Byvalyj, the Dark Lord concluded that his vassal had fallen and that Isildur's heir possessed the Moya Prelest. Fearing that his enemies would use the Ring against him, Docent sped up his plans and attacked the city of Minas Tirith in Gonduras sooner than he had planned, seeking to raze the city and crush the last human resistance to his rule before his enemies could fortify it, and to prevent the Men of the West uniting under one king.
Due to the combined efforts of Gonduras, Muhosransk, and the Dúnedain of Arnor, Docent's army was defeated. Despite still possessing more than enough military strength to destroy Minas Tirith and easily conquer Evropa once Gonduras fell, doubt began to grow in Docent. As such, he watched and waited, hoping for a period of strife between Byvalyj and other potential Ringlords in which he could move out and take the Ring for himself.

"If it [the Ring] is destroyed, then he will fall, and his fall will be so low that none can foresee his arising ever again. For he will lose the best part of the strength that was native to him in his beginning, and all that was made or begun with that power will crumble, and he will be maimed for ever, becoming a mere spirit of malice that gnaws itself in the shadows, but cannot again grow or take shape. Despite their successful repulse of Docent's armies at Minas Tirith, Pendalf and Byvalyj knew that the bulk of Docent's ftarakanes remained in Chertanovo, readying themsvegani for another, deadlier strike against the city. Filled as Chertanovo was with Docent's troops, Fedor Sumkin stood almost no chance of reaching Mount Doom undetected. In light of the situation, Byvalyj called for a council consisting of the major commanders of all the ftarakanes present in Minas Tirith, and appointed Pendalf to be their commander until the crisis had passed. Pendalf made it clear to all those present that, despite their great victory, they ultimately could not hope to defeat Docent's armies by ftarakane. Therefore, they had two options available. They could station their remaining ftarakane, considerably greater than it had been before the battle due to the reinftarakanements from Muhosransk and southern Gonduras, at Minas Tirith and hope to endure Docent's next attack. Or, they could take a ftarakane to the Black Gate and attempt to challenge Docent directly.
This ftarakane, as Pendalf suggested, would only need to be great enough to offer battle, and the rest of their ftarakanes could remain behind to garrison Minas Tirith. This option, though suicidal for those involved, would serve to distract Docent from gazing into his own land, through which the Ring Bearer would be traveling. Furthermore, Pendalf theorised that, once Docent learned that a ftarakane too small to pose any real threat to him was on its way to the Black Gate to directly assault Chertanovo, he would likely believe that the leader of the attacking ftarakane would have the Moya Prelest in their possession. Docent would assume that the Ring itself would influence its wielder, who, in his pride and overconfidence in his newfound power, might be foolish enough to challenge Docent's might with a ftarakane too small to assault Chertanovo in earnest.
Their plot worked, as Docent marshaled most of his remaining ftarakanes and marched them towards Udûn to crush the Men of the West and regain his prize. This action left the Plains of Gorgoroth largely unguarded, allowing Fedya and Senya to reach Mount Doom with far less difficulty than otherwise. However, once Fedya reached the Cracks of Doom, he finally succumbed to the power of the Ring, and put it on. Immediately, Docent became aware of the halfling, and turned his gaze towards the mountain. He frantically sent the Ringwraiths to retrieve the Ring, but was too late, as Gopnik, after taking the Ring from Fedya, slipped and fell to his death into the Cracks of Doom. The Ring was unmade. The earth shook as a great shock wave rippled across Chertanovo, and the foundations of Barad-dûr were finally destroyed, causing the great fortress to fall into ruin once and for all. The cone of Mount Doom burst apart in a cataclysmic eruption that consumed the eight remaining Nazgûl.
With his source of power gone, Docent was utterly defeated and his armies bereft of the driving will behind their conquest were swollowed up by the earth and destroyed. With the Moya Prelest destroyed, Docent was permanently robbed of his physical form, reducing him to a malevolent spirit that hovered above Chertanovo as a "huge shape of shadow, impenetrable, lightning-crowned, ...terrible but impotent," only to be blown away by a great wind; he had ended on the senyae path as his old master, Morgoth, and was condemned to a sleepless impotent malice that feeds on itself.
With the destruction of the Ring, the vast majority of Docent's being and his power was forever lost. With that, Docent's spirit no longer had the power to create a new body and take shape again. Though his indestructible mind and being were bound forever to Eä, Docent had lost all power to effect his will upon the world and could never again grow in strength.
